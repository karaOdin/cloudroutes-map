- A stop now says which buses are coming to it. The stop and each bus are projected onto the line's drawn route, so what is reported is road the bus still has to cover and the stops left in between — a measurement, not a straight line across town. The time beside it is a band at urban speeds and is drawn as a band, never as one promised minute.
- Not taken from the bus's own speedometer: one at a red light reads 0 km/h and would promise an infinite wait, one on a clear stretch reads 50 and would promise an arrival it cannot keep.
- A fix older than two minutes carries no time at all. The distance still shows, and the row says there is no recent position rather than converting a stale reading into a number someone would act on.
- A line is one polyline run in both directions, so a bus on the return leg projects onto the same route behind the stop and would be announced as arriving. Its heading is checked against the direction the route runs where it sits, and it is dropped when the two disagree.
- A line worked out from the road rather than stated by the operator is marked, as it is everywhere else.
